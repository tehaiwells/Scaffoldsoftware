# Remaining acceptance gaps and operating limits

This is a working local demonstration of the core round trip and stock control. It is not a claim that every detail of the full commercial V1 brief is complete.

1. Yard lists (multi-line load lists) now exist: the office lists several products for one site, the yard reserves exact packs line by line (all-or-nothing per line, short lines are reported with the reason and can be re-planned), the truck loads, and the same list travels with the truck as the delivery docket with the sent quantities frozen at departure. Lines cannot be edited after creation — cancel the list and re-list. A line whose loading task is cancelled after planning becomes PARTIALLY ALLOCATED and blocks departure until the list is cancelled and re-listed. The older single-product request form remains as a quick one-line request.
2. The main yard view currently opens the first company yard. The API supports additional yards, but a multiple-yard selector and guided multi-step onboarding UI remain. Sites start as a 20 × 16 m rectangle and can be redrawn from the SITES page with the same segment editor as the yard, including height, loading zone and gate. The yard can be resized at any time: stillages that would fall outside the new boundary, sit on the loading zone or exceed a lowered height are moved to clear ground inside it (stacks are set down), each move recorded as a RELOCATED ledger event; crew left outside or under a moved stillage are re-seated; the loading zone and gate fall back to a fitting spot. Resizing works with workers and forklifts present: mounted forklifts move with their drivers, manual drives and walks are stopped (loads stay on the forks), and in-flight automatic movements are re-planned against the new boundary. A resize is refused only when the area is too small for its stock or a stillage is currently on handling equipment. The gate is drawn on the plan but does not yet drive truck entry routing and is not kept clear of stock.
3. Routing is a conservative 500 mm grid with sampled swept footprint checks. It can reject a geometrically possible narrow route. One machine route runs per handling area to avoid conflicting moving loads. The machine/worker graphics are symbolic state displays, not continuous real-time physics. Detailed operator walking-to-machine paths, arbitrary manual-handling profiles and measured 30 FPS rendering remain. Manual hand carrying is not automatically allowed.
4. Simulation timing is a company-level configuration shared by its yards/sites. Crane resource counts are separate, but pickup and placement share the configured demo step time. Per-machine timing and full reach/support rating models need further work.
5. The yard plan has zoom (buttons or scroll), 45-degree rotation, drag-to-pan and a 3D (isometric) toggle that draws stillages as stacked boxes with their contents on hover and on the cards; the view is remembered per browser. It is a projected drawing, not a physics scene. The browser supports click-to-move and HTML container-card drag/drop; direct dragging of the SVG yard shape, custom ghost placement previews, tablet touch dragging and broad accessibility/device validation remain. Native browser drag ghosts are used. Exact placement and 90-degree orientation are available in the click alternative.
6. Stock views and container visuals are limited to 100 containers per page. Company totals and CSV exports cover all scoped stock. Stock search searches the current page; server-side global catalogue search/category filters remain. The STOCK page now has a per-product materials register (yard / sites / trucks / reserved / available), an added-stock list (opening balances and purchases) and a removed-stock list (sales and disposals); removals refuse reserved, in-transit and count-locked stock. Register and list CSV exports cover all scoped stock. The API still reads a company's object records to produce a page, so this is not a claim of million-row scale readiness. History uses indexed cursor pagination; CSV history is capped at 10,000 rows.
7. The persistence layer uses relational tenant/membership/content/ledger tables plus versioned JSON domain objects. References between JSON objects are validated by domain commands, not all by SQL foreign keys. A fully normalized production schema and PostgreSQL adapter/migration remain; PostgreSQL has not been tested.
8. Secure local sessions and role boundaries work. Password change/reset/recovery, membership removal, role editing, invite acceptance, distributed throttling and independent security review remain before public deployment. Financial permission is reserved; no financial screens/data exist.
9. Company records in this application are simulation workspaces only. There is no live-mode switch. Future real operations must use a separate confirmation adapter and explicit authorized confirmations, never the simulation timer. Do not mix real operational records into demo companies.
10. No manufacturer catalogue has been verified. No certified stacking, lift, load restraint, axle-load or road-legality calculations are provided. See CATALOGUE_PROVENANCE.md.

Known safe failure behavior: missing equipment/path/weight produces an explanation; busy equipment queues. Cancel before pickup releases reservations. After pickup, finish/retry valid placement; no teleporting undo exists. A blocked post-pickup task retains its cargo and resources. Automatic relocation planning and an alternate-destination recovery editor remain; configuration/path problems must be resolved before retrying.


## Yard overview update
The home page lists configured simulation workers and equipment, their assigned tasks and current availability. These resources are distinct from login accounts. The side parking apron is a dimensioned layout outside the storage polygon; it does not add storage area or implement truck manoeuvring, swept paths or parking reservations. Its initial 10 m by 3.1 m preview uses the requested 6 m by 2.1 m deck, an assumed 3 m cab and 0.5 m clearance on each side. Overall vehicle dimensions and clearance can be saved per yard. Existing truck deck records are preserved. Cages are separately styled mesh frames with editable dimensions; their suggested dimensions are illustrative.


## Worker controls
Workers support select, move, stop, hold and return-to-work orders. Manual movement is server-owned, uses a 0.5 m footprint and 1.4 m/s speed, respects stock obstacles and yard boundaries, and pauses with the simulation. A manually moved worker holds at arrival until returned to automatic work. Assigned handling workers cannot be redirected mid-task. These controls do not yet assign specific packing/loading jobs to a chosen worker, simulate crowd avoidance between workers, or animate operators boarding equipment.


## Forklift mounting
Configured yard forklifts now have a parked footprint and a worker mount/dismount flow. Mount reserves the forklift and walks to its boarding point before attaching the worker. Stop cancels an approach and releases the reservation. A mounted worker and forklift are excluded from automatic jobs; dismount before returning to automatic work. Mounted drivers can now drive, pick up and place ground-level stillages in the yard. Forklift drawings use illustrative dimensions.


## Manual forklift handling
Right-click ground drives a mounted forklift. Select a ground-level stillage and use Pick up; use Place load followed by a ground click to place. Pickup approaches from the left (forks face right); turning radius, stacking and truck loading are not part of these manual controls. Stillages support 0/90 degree placement orientations with conservative rotation clearance. Driving uses a conservative rectangular footprint for forklift plus load. A blocked move stops without releasing cargo. Stop cancels an approach but keeps any carried load. Ground pickup and placement are persisted with ledger events, and manual cargo cannot be edited or assigned to another job. Manual routing does not model pedestrian avoidance.


## Home stockpile (icon grids)
The Home page shows every enabled catalogue component as an icon in four grids: AtPac, Quickstage, Tube & Clip and Miscellaneous (accessories, tools and "other" from every system, plus any product from a system outside the three). A step row (−100 … +100) sets how many pieces one click adds or takes out. Adding uses the same intake rule as before (first available stillage, then a new stillage on the shortest pile) and is recorded as a purchase or original stock. Taking out removes from the highest, most recently added stillage that can be reached, records a STOCK_REMOVED ledger line with the chosen reason, and refuses stock that is reserved, under a stocktake, on a forklift or buried under another stillage. An emptied stillage stays in the yard (it is reused by the next add, or removed with −1 empty stillage). The icons are generic category glyphs, not manufacturer drawings.

## Yard fixtures
The yard editor can place truck entry and exit lanes, a toilet, a yard office and custom fixtures as rectangles inside the boundary. Lanes are kept clear of stock only; solid fixtures also block worker and forklift routing and parking spots. Saving fixtures over stored stillages relocates them the same way a boundary change does. The entry and exit lanes are drawn with direction arrows but trucks still park on the side apron; truck driving through the lanes, swept paths and gate control are not simulated.

## Layout planner
The Yard page has a layout planner: drag a stillage to a new spot, or drop it onto another stillage to stack it; whole piles move together and R turns the load while dragging. Check plan shows the ordered instructions; Commit this plan sends them to the crew as a chain of single forklift moves executed in order by the automatic engine (one active plan per yard). The sequencer only picks what is on top and only places where the spot and its supporting stillage are ready; a swap or a pile move sets one stillage down on clear ground first. It refuses plans that leave a stillage on a moving one, overlap, exceed 7 high or the storage height, or have no clear forklift lane. A step can still block at execution time if something changes (the task shows the reason and can be retried); Cancel remaining steps cancels unpicked steps from the end, and a step already on the forklift is placed first. Drag-and-drop is pointer based and has not been validated on touch devices.

## Precise placement and rotation
Place load now opens a dedicated planner with a full loaded-footprint preview, exact integer-millimetre X/Y coordinates, 1/10/50/100/500/1000 mm snap options, nudge buttons and 0/90 degree rotation. Coordinates anchor the top-left of the rotated footprint. Map clicks pin a preview; only Confirm placement moves the forklift. Read-only server validation checks actual stock, yard geometry, route and conservative rotation clearance. Confirmation and arrival revalidate obstacles. Rotation is a simulation state change at placement, not articulated fork or load physics; the access envelope conservatively covers both orientations.
